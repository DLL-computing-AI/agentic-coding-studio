Research

Studying how young people learn with coding agents.

The project examines the learning processes, outcomes, and contexts that emerge when youth direct AI coding agents to create computational artifacts. These are research questions—not findings.

Three areas of inquiry

Learning, outcomes, and context.

01

Learning Processes

How do students learn to plan, prompt, inspect, test, revise, and reflect while using AI coding agents to create games, stories, and apps?

02

Learning Outcomes

How does participation relate to computational agency, AI literacy, reasoning about AI-generated code and assets, STEM identity, and motivation?

03

Learning Context

How do educators, community, professional development, and platform design help or hinder students’ learning of agentic coding?

Conceptual foundations

Learning through meaningful participation and creation.

Situated Learning

Learning through participation in authentic computational practices.

Cognitive Apprenticeship

Using modeling, scaffolding, coaching, reflection, and gradual transfer of responsibility.

Constructionism

Learning through creating personally meaningful, shareable artifacts.

Computational Agency

Intentionally directing computational tools and AI toward meaningful goals while evaluating and revising the resulting work.

Approach

Iterative research in real learning contexts.

The project uses design-based implementation research (DBIR) and mixed methods, with cycles of co-design, implementation, data collection, analysis, and refinement.

Possible data sources include youth projects, AI interaction logs, platform data, observations, educator and youth interviews, learning activities, and DBIR process artifacts.
